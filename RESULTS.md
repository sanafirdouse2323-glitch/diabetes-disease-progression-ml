## 3. Split-conformal prediction intervals

The conformal procedure used absolute residual scores and the finite-sample rank
`k = ceil((n_cal + 1) * (1 - alpha))`. If this rank exceeds the number of calibration observations, the threshold is positive infinity, as required for the stated finite-sample construction.

| Model | Nominal marginal coverage | Observed test coverage | Mean interval width |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mean Baseline | 90% | 93.3% | 240.433 |
| Linear Regression | 90% | 84.3% | 164.600 |
| Ridge | 90% | 89.9% | 181.196 |
| Lasso | 90% | 88.8% | 170.445 |
| Random Forest | 90% | 94.4% | 190.048 |
| PyTorch MLP (16, 8) | 90% | 89.9% | 219.819 |
| TensorFlow MLP (16, 8) | 90% | 94.4% | 269.726 |

The finite-sample conformal statement is a **marginal coverage guarantee over exchangeable calibration and future test observations**. It is not a guarantee that every individual model/test split will display at least 90% empirical coverage, nor a 90% probability statement about a particular person. The empirical values above are the observed coverage on one held-out set of 89 observations; Linear Regression and Lasso are below the 90% nominal level on this realized test partition. They are not hidden or rounded up.

### Attribution method interpretation

* The Ridge coefficients and Random Forest mean-decrease-in-impurity values above summarize the fitted models. They describe associations in this benchmark, not causal effects.
* For a linear model, baseline-relative contributions are calculated as the fitted coefficient multiplied by the feature deviation from the model-fitting reference mean. Their sum matches the model's prediction difference from the reference profile, up to floating-point precision.
* For the Random Forest demo, local contributions use one-feature-at-a-time perturbations to the reference value. Because feature interactions and correlations can cause overlap, these contributions are not an additive decomposition and should not be read as SHAP values.
* The implementation does not include SHAP, neural input-gradient attribution, or causal inference.

### Matched-sample comparison and interpretation

With the fitting sample size held at 225 observations, the best classical models had mean test MAE around 42.9, compared with 53.964 for PyTorch and 57.204 for TensorFlow. The classical models also had lower mean RMSE and higher mean R². The neural models performed better than the mean baseline, but did not outperform any of the four classical regressors (Linear Regression, Lasso, Ridge, or Random Forest) on the mean point-prediction metrics under these fixed settings.

These results do not establish that classical models are universally superior. No formal significance tests or neural hyperparameter search were performed. The repeated outer test partitions overlap, so the across-seed standard deviations describe split sensitivity rather than independent-sample confidence intervals. The MLPs' results also reflect the selected architecture and training budget, not all possible neural configurations.

### Interpretation and limits

- The original single-split table earlier in this document is retained as a historical one-seed result; it is not replaced by the repeated-seed averages.
- Repeated random hold-out sets overlap, so these results characterize sensitivity to split choice, not independent-sample uncertainty, population-level generalization, or external validation.
- The benchmark remains small, preprocessed, and educational. These results do not establish clinical utility or support patient-level decisions.

## 8. Controlled validation-selected PyTorch hyperparameter study

A separate controlled study investigated whether the original PyTorch MLP result was sensitive to a small, predeclared set of training settings. It did not alter the original benchmark or select settings using test results.

- Workflow run: [Repeated-seed neural hyperparameter study](https://github.com/sanafirdouse2323-glitch/diabetes-disease-progression-ml/actions/runs/43000943283)
- Repository commit for paired comparison: `62f4ba7a9f074184e25ecc8ce292f1310fd18198`
- Dataset: `sklearn.datasets.load_diabetes`, 442 observations and 10 preprocessed features.
- Seeds: 42–51 (10 outer splits).
- Per seed: 225 fitting, 57 validation, 71 calibration, and 89 test observations.
- Candidate grid: baseline learning rate 0.001/dropout 0.10; learning rates 0.0003 and 0.003; dropout 0.30 and 0.00, with architecture (16, 8) and batch size 32 fixed.
- Selection: minimum validation MSE within each seed. Calibration was used only after selection. Test observations were used only for final evaluation.
- The uploaded `repeated-seed-neural-tuning` artifact contains candidate validation metrics, selected per-seed metrics (including same-test-split default control metrics), summary metrics, training histories, and metadata.

### Point-prediction comparison

| Model | Test MAE (mean ± SD) | Test RMSE (mean ± SD) | Test R² (mean ± SD) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Original default PyTorch MLP (paired control) | 53.963 ± 3.984 | 69.300 ± 4.493 | 0.127 ± 0.124 |
| Validation-selected PyTorch MLP | **44.934 ± 4.931** | **56.790 ± 6.019** | **0.410 ± 0.132** |

The mean paired MAE change (selected minus default) was **−9.030** points; it was negative on all ten seeds (range −16.246 to −4.287). The higher learning rate (0.003) was selected in all ten seeds; dropout remained 0.10 and batch size 32. The mean selected-model empirical test coverage was 91.2% (SD 3.6 percentage points), with mean interval width 198.229. The default-control point metrics are evaluated on the same test observations as the selected model for each seed. Test metrics did not influence configuration selection.

### Interpretation and limitations

This paired result supports the narrower conclusion that the original PyTorch MLP's performance on this benchmark was sensitive to the tested learning-rate setting. The selected model's mean MAE (44.934) is substantially closer to the matched classical results (Linear Regression 42.899; Lasso 42.927; Random Forest 44.858; Ridge 49.015) than the default MLP was. It does not show that the neural model is better than classical methods: its mean MAE remains slightly worse than Linear Regression and Lasso and similar to Random Forest. No formal statistical test was performed. The five-setting search is limited, validation-based selection can be noisy, and the repeated outer test partitions overlap; the across-seed SDs and paired changes are descriptive split-sensitivity summaries, not independent-sample confidence intervals. This is not external validation or evidence of clinical utility.

## 9. Limitations and interpretation


1. **Small, preprocessed benchmark:** 442 observations and 10 preprocessed features are insufficient to establish clinical utility, transportability, or robust subgroup fairness.
2. **Repeated hold-out limitations:** The ten-seed analyses use overlapping random test partitions. Their standard deviations are descriptive split-sensitivity measures, not independent-sample confidence intervals. Repeated nested validation and external validation are still needed.
3. **No clinical validation:** This work is an educational/research software demonstration, not a diagnostic or clinical decision-support system.
4. **Subgroup analysis is narrow:** Only the two encoded values of the standardized `sex` feature are compared. Age-group analyses and broader fairness evaluations are not included in this measured run.
5. **Interpretability scope:** The implemented methods are coefficients, Random Forest impurity importances, and local perturbation-style explanations. This run does not implement SHAP or causal inference.
6. **Limited neural search:** The original PyTorch/TensorFlow baseline comparison uses fixed settings. The separate PyTorch follow-up tests only five predeclared learning-rate/dropout configurations for one fixed MLP architecture; it is not an exhaustive search, and its ranking should not be generalized to all neural architectures or training strategies.

## 11. Nested repeated-CV comparison and inner-fold family selection

A dedicated benchmark compares seven regression model families plus a mean-prediction baseline on the full `sklearn.datasets.load_diabetes` dataset (442 observations; 10 preprocessed features). It uses five outer folds repeated three times and three shuffled inner folds within each outer training partition. This produces 15 outer-fold evaluations per candidate. Each outer training partition contains 353 or 354 observations; the corresponding outer test fold contains 89 or 88 observations.

For each family, hyperparameters are selected using inner-fold mean absolute error (MAE), and the selected estimator is refitted on the full outer training partition. Scale-sensitive estimators use a `StandardScaler` inside a scikit-learn `Pipeline`, ensuring that each scaler is learned only from the relevant inner training fold. The outer-fold targets are not used for hyperparameter or family selection.

- Workflow run: [Nested tabular benchmark](https://github.com/sanafirdouse2323-glitch/diabetes-disease-progression-ml/actions/runs/43006717716)
- Source commit tested: `0fb5463f88f3bff4855f0977b6f4d33aef20fb3d`
- The workflow artifact contains per-fold scores, per-family summaries, selected-family results and frequencies, and benchmark metadata.

### Evaluation of selecting a model family

To avoid declaring a winner by inspecting the outer-fold results, the experiment separately evaluates the *family-selection procedure*. In each outer training partition, it selects the candidate family with the lowest inner-CV MAE, then evaluates that family’s refitted estimator on the untouched outer fold.

| Selection-procedure metric | Mean ± SD across 15 outer folds |
|---|---:|
| Test MAE | 44.854 ± 3.105 |
| Test RMSE | 55.565 ± 3.677 |
| Test R² | 0.469 ± 0.079 |

The selected-family frequencies were: RBF-SVR 10/15 folds (66.7%), Ridge 2/15 (13.3%), Extra Trees 1/15 (6.7%), ElasticNet 1/15 (6.7%), and Linear Regression 1/15 (6.7%). The mean baseline, Random Forest, and Histogram Gradient Boosting were not selected.

The selector's mean outer-fold MAE (44.854) was not lower than the fixed Linear Regression strategy's per-family mean (44.483). This does not mean inner-fold selection is invalid; it shows that, for this candidate set and dataset, adding a model-family selection step did not demonstrate an improvement over using the simple linear strategy. The selector's performance and the per-family summaries answer different questions and should be reported separately.

### Interpretation and limits

- The experiment uses a single small, public, preprocessed benchmark. It is not external validation and does not establish performance on a new hospital, population, or clinical workflow.
- The candidate grids are compact and predeclared, not exhaustive.
- The repeated outer-fold results are dependent because observations recur across repeats. No inferential claim is made from their standard deviations, and no formal significance test is reported.
- This nested benchmark does not fit conformal prediction intervals. Split-conformal results in earlier sections belong to separate experiments and should not be conflated with this nested model comparison.
- Predictive results do not establish clinical utility, fairness, causal explanation, or readiness for patient-level use.
